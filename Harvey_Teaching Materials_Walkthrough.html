Design ruleStart with the learning need—not “What can Harvey make?”. Treat outputs as drafts for checking and adaptation, not as resources ready for direct release.
1

Plan the educational use before generating anything.

Define what students or educators should be able to do with the material, then preserve the reading, reasoning, synthesis, research or judgement students are meant to practise.

Use Harvey to generate and refine drafts within an educator-led design process. A polished output is not automatically a good teaching resource.

Purpose

Define the learning need

Prepare for class, distinguish authorities, practise issue identification, apply doctrine, compare reasoning, test understanding or prepare feedback.

Product

Choose a bounded material type

Case teaching brief, seminar-question sequence, issue map, worked-example scaffold, revision activity, lecture-note update, rubric draft or feedback framework.

Boundary

Decide what students still do

Do not create a resource that silently supplies the intellectual work students are expected to demonstrate themselves.

2

Ground the task in appropriate sources.

For current law, do not rely on model memory. Use current primary or authoritative materials, a suitable Knowledge Source, or another defined source where available.

Source discipline

Use the smallest adequate source set

Use Vault where the task genuinely involves a larger collection or repeated analysis across a corpus.

Governance

Apply institutional data controls

For student work, default to de-identified or approved material unless identifiable data is expressly permitted.

!
Copyright, privacy and confidentiality are governance questions

Harvey's security controls do not remove the need to follow University data-classification rules, licence conditions, assessment procedures and professional-confidentiality obligations.

3

Write a high-signal prompt.

Use request, context and output. Harvey's CLAIM framework—Context, Legal task, Audience, Instructions and Mode of output—can be adapted for teaching by adding the learning purpose.

ContextUnit, topic, sources, jurisdiction and setting.
Legal taskSummarise, compare, critique, extract, draft, classify, sequence or revise.
AudienceCohort or staff group and assumed legal knowledge.
InstructionsSource limits, exclusions, verification and pedagogical constraints.
Mode + learning purposeReviewable output plus the thinking it should elicit.

i
Improve is a drafting aid

If Harvey's Improve function rewrites a rough prompt, read it before running and restore any teaching, jurisdictional or source constraints that matter.

4

Generate, verify and revise in Assistant.

Ask for structured drafts, break complex work into stages, verify source grounding and revise for legal accuracy, teaching fit, accessibility, timing and tone.

1
Create the first draft

Attach relevant files or select intended Knowledge Sources and ask for a structured output rather than an undefined “answer”.

2
Break complex tasks into stages

Extract propositions → check sources → generate questions → revise cognitive demand.

3
Check source grounding

Verify every important case reference, statutory reference, quotation, proposition and jurisdictional claim. Citations may not appear for every assertion or inference.

4
Check teaching fit

Does the material align with the intended learning outcome, activate the intended thinking and preserve productive difficulty?

5
Revise the artefact

Edit for disciplinary accuracy, local terminology, accessibility, class timing and tone. Convert passive handouts into activities where useful.

6
Retain a short record where warranted

For reusable, assessment-facing or higher-risk resources, record sources, changes, verification and reuse constraints.

!
Human verification is not optional for legal content

A fluent output is not evidence that the law, quotation, citation or doctrinal characterisation is correct.

5

Use templates that preserve the learning task.

These patterns make source use, learning purpose and educator review visible rather than generating complete student answers by default.

Case teaching brief

Context: I am preparing [CLASS / SEMINAR] for [UNIT] students studying [TOPIC] in [JURISDICTION].
Sources: Use only the attached judgment and any expressly selected Knowledge Source.
Audience: [FIRST-YEAR LLB / LATER-YEAR LLB / JD / LLM].
Learning purpose: help students identify how the court moved from issue to reasoning to holding; do not replace reading of the judgment.

Task: Prepare a concise case teaching brief with these headings:
1. Procedural history and posture
2. Material facts
3. Legal issue(s)
4. Holding / orders relevant to the issue
5. Court's reasoning
6. Key authorities relied upon
7. Doctrinal significance and any limits
8. Two questions for class discussion
9. Matters requiring educator verification

Instructions: Distinguish the court's reasoning from your own inference. Do not invent authorities or quotations. Cite the source passage where available and flag uncertainty.

Seminar discussion questions

Context: Prepare a sequenced set of seminar questions for [UNIT] on [TOPIC] in [JURISDICTION].
Audience: [STUDENT COHORT].
Sources: Use only [SPECIFY MATERIALS].
Learning purpose: move students from comprehension to application, comparison and evaluation.

Provide:
1. One opening retrieval/comprehension question
2. Two application questions using a short variation or hypothetical
3. One comparison or evaluation question
4. One likely misconception and a question designed to expose it
5. One closing consolidation or transfer activity

For each item, identify the source, doctrine or reasoning move it is intended to activate. Do not provide model answers unless separately requested for the educator version.

Update lecture notes with current law

Compare the supplied lecture notes with the supplied current case/statutory materials for [UNIT] in [JURISDICTION].

Task:
1. Identify passages that may now require revision.
2. Explain why revision may be needed.
3. Suggest revised wording using [DELETE: ...] and [ADD: ...] markers.
4. Cite the supporting source and pinpoint reference where available.
5. Classify each suggestion as: substantive legal update / clarification / teaching improvement.
6. Flag anything that cannot be verified from the supplied sources.

Do not rewrite unaffected material or assume legal developments outside the source set.

Problem-question or revision activity

Create a student practice activity for [UNIT / TOPIC] in [JURISDICTION].
Audience: [COHORT].
Learning outcome: [ULO OR CAPABILITY].
Source base: [MATERIALS].

Produce:
- a short problem or scenario;
- an issue-identification scaffold;
- 3–5 prompts that require students to explain their reasoning;
- a source-checking step; and
- an educator-only debrief identifying intended issues and common misconceptions.

Do not provide a complete student-facing model answer. Keep the debrief separate and clearly labelled EDUCATOR ONLY.
i
Why a case brief rather than a generic IRAC summary?

IRAC is principally a problem-solving structure. A case brief organised around procedural posture, issues, holding and reasons is usually more faithful to how judgments operate. Use IRAC where the learning purpose is explicitly to practise that schema.

6

Check the teaching artefact before release or reuse.

Legal accuracy is necessary but not sufficient. The material must also be traceable, aligned, cognitively appropriate, usable, accessible and respectful of assessment boundaries.

Product-accuracy notes from the August 2026 revision
  • Assistant: default drafting workspace for asking, analysing, drafting, uploading documents, selecting Knowledge Sources, revising and exporting.
  • Current law: explicitly ground teaching materials in current primary or authoritative sources rather than assuming model knowledge is current.
  • Citations: linked citations assist verification but may not appear for every assertion or inference.
  • Prompting: current guidance supports request + context + output and Harvey's CLAIM framework.
  • Privacy: PII means personally identifiable information; permitted use remains an institutional governance question.
  • Naming: use “Harvey”, not “HarveyAI”, for the current public product name.
