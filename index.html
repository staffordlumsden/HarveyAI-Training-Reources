Start with the educational task

Use the smallest Harvey feature that adequately supports the work.

These resources are designed around a common principle: Harvey can augment human-led educational work by structuring, extracting, comparing and drafting material for review. It should not displace legal judgement, academic judgement, source checking, assessment decisions or local governance.

A defensible working approach

The five resources are not a mandatory sequence. Use this four-step logic to decide what you need and how far to take it.

1

Define the purpose

State the educational task, audience, jurisdiction, source needs and the capability or decision that must remain human-led.

2

Choose the smallest adequate feature

Start in Assistant for bounded one-off work; move to Library, Workflow agents, Review Tables or Vault only when reuse, structure or scale warrants it.

3

Verify the output

Check source grounding, legal currency, quotations, citations, classifications and pedagogical fit before relying on the material.

4

Govern reuse and sharing

Decide who should see the prompt, workflow, corpus or output; record ownership and review triggers where the resource will persist.

!
Product security and educational permission are different questions.

Harvey's platform controls do not by themselves determine whether University, student, confidential or assessment-related material may be uploaded or shared. Apply the relevant local data, privacy, copyright, assessment and AI-use requirements to the specific task.
