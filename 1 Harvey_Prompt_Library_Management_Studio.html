Design principleA saved prompt is a reusable instruction, not a finished curriculum resource. Make the task, sources, boundaries and human review points visible.
1

Start with the educational purpose.

Define what the prompt is for before deciding how to phrase it. A reusable prompt should have a clear teaching or educator-workflow purpose, intended user, legal context and source boundary.

Library is a curated repository of reusable instructions. Treat it as a starting point for disciplined reuse, not as a substitute for educational design or legal verification. A prompt is ready to save or share only when its purpose, intended user, legal context, source boundary, output requirements and review responsibility are clear.

1 · Educational purpose

What should this prompt support?

Examples include seminar preparation, issue-spotting practice, authority checking, assessment design and feedback drafting.

2 · Intended user

Who should use the output?

Decide whether the user is an educator, student, clinic or other professional user, then specify what they should—and should not—receive.

3 · Legal context

Localise before reuse.

Name the jurisdiction, area of law, unit, cohort and level where relevant. Do not assume a Harvey-supplied prompt is already suitable for Australian legal education.

4 · Source boundary

Decide what may ground the response.

Choose uploaded material, a selected Knowledge Source, a Vault, or information supplied in the prompt. Prefer grounded sources for legal propositions.

Harvey's CLAIM framework — adapted for legal education

CContext

Unit, cohort, legal topic, factual or documentary context, and educational purpose.

LLegal task

Use a precise verb: summarise, compare, extract, issue-spot, critique, classify, draft or generate questions.

AAudience

Who will use the output and at what level: educator, LLB student, JD student, clinic supervisor, etc.

IInstructions

Jurisdiction, sources, assumptions, exclusions, verification requirements, tone and educational boundary.

MMode

Specify a table, outline, issue list, chronology, checklist, questions, feedback comments or other reviewable form.

Naming convention

[Audience] — [Task] — [Area of law] — [Version]

Use a consistent convention so prompts can be found, maintained and retired. The page will generate a suggested title from your current context in the final step.

3

Draft a reusable prompt with visible boundaries.

Choose the smallest useful category, then generate a structured prompt from your local context. The output should support the intended capability rather than bypass it.

✓
Educational design question

Does the output help the learner or educator perform a reviewable step, or does it accidentally supply the performance that the task is intended to develop or assess?

Reusable CLAIM prompt

Edit freely. If the context changes, use Regenerate to rebuild the template.

Verification language: make “check” observable

Weak: “Make sure this is accurate.”
No defined source boundary, no uncertainty handling and no indication of what a reviewer should inspect.
Better: identify propositions and supporting passages.
“Using the attached judgment only, identify each legal proposition and the passage that supports it. Flag any proposition you cannot support from the judgment.”
Best for teaching use: separate source content, synthesis and educator review.
“Using the attached judgment, produce the requested teaching output. Separate the court's reasoning from your synthesis, identify any uncertainty, and list the points the educator should verify before using the material with students.”
4

Test legal validity and pedagogical validity separately.

A prompt can be legally plausible but pedagogically poor—or pedagogically appealing but legally unreliable. Test both before treating it as a reusable local asset.

Run a controlled test.

Use a known case, statute, problem or de-identified sample that you can independently check. Test the prompt in the same way the intended user will use it.

Baseline output
Check legal validity.

Verify propositions, authorities, quotations and jurisdiction against primary or otherwise approved sources. Treat source links as checking aids, not proof.

Legally checked
Check pedagogical validity.

Ask whether the output supports the intended learning outcome. For student-facing prompts, ensure it scaffolds reasoning rather than supplying the assessed performance by default.

Pedagogically coherent
Stress-test the boundary.

Try ambiguous facts, missing sources, a neighbouring jurisdiction and a request that exceeds the intended task. The prompt should flag unsupported expansion.

Robust boundary
Revise for observable output.

Replace vague verbs such as “discuss” or “help” with concrete deliverables, fields and limits.

Clearer prompt
Record the version.

Keep owner, audience, version/date, source assumptions, test case and unresolved limitations with the prompt or in the School's governance record.

5

Use examples to show what “education-ready” means.

The strongest examples localise jurisdiction, define the audience and source set, specify an observable output, and make the educational boundary and verification responsibility explicit.

Generic
“Summarise this case for my class.”
Education-ready

Using the attached Australian judgment or selected Australian legal Knowledge Source, prepare a concise teaching summary of [case name] for [cohort] in [unit]. Use the headings: procedural history; material facts; legal issues; decision/orders; ratio and reasoning; significant dicta (if any); authorities relied upon; and three discussion questions. Do not introduce authorities that are not supported by the source set. Flag any point that requires checking against the judgment.

Generic
“Create discussion questions about contract law.”
Education-ready

Draft six seminar questions for [cohort] in Australian Contract Law on [topic]. Sequence them from comprehension to application and evaluation. At least two questions must require students to use the assigned case or statute. Do not provide model answers. For the educator only, add one likely misconception and one follow-up prompt that would expose it.

Generic
“Help mark this student answer.”
Education-ready

Using the supplied rubric and the de-identified student response only, map observable evidence in the response to each rubric criterion. Draft concise formative feedback and one feed-forward action per criterion for educator review. Do not infer missing evidence, invent a mark, or make a final grade decision. Flag any judgement that requires educator moderation.

Generic
“Help the student solve this problem question.”
Education-ready

For student practice, identify the legal issues raised by the problem and list the facts relevant to each issue. Name the legal concepts or sources the student should investigate, but do not apply the law to reach a conclusion and do not draft a model answer. End with three questions the student should answer before writing.

6

Govern visibility, ownership and maintenance.

A good prompt library is maintained, not merely populated. Separate local audience decisions from what Harvey's product permissions actually guarantee.

!
Do not assume “faculty” or “student” is a Harvey visibility boundary.

Harvey's public documentation confirms that Library admins can manage workspace prompts and can hide Harvey-supplied prompts from a team; it does not establish a universal educator/student segmentation model. Confirm local permissions and sharing settings before publishing a student-facing collection.

Sharing

Keep early or high-risk prompts narrow.

Keep them personal or within a small staff group until legal and pedagogical testing is complete. Share more broadly only after a deliberate visibility decision.

Ownership

Assign a named staff owner.

The owner should review the prompt when law, policy, unit design, Knowledge Sources or Harvey platform behaviour changes.

Metadata

Keep enough information to maintain it.

Record title, audience, owner, version/date, jurisdiction, intended source set, test status and restrictions.

Admin features

Use documented controls where appropriate.

Workspace admins can manage organisation prompts; Harvey documents edit/delete controls and the ability to hide Harvey-supplied prompts from the team. Library admins can bulk-upload up to 500 prompts by CSV.
